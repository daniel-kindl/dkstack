# Assessment

Find what the learner can do without help, and record it as a level for each concept.

## Levels

Give a level to each concept, not to a whole subject.

| Level | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| 0 Unfamiliar | The learner showed no understanding yet. |
| 1 Recognition | The learner recognizes basic terms or ideas. |
| 2 Comprehension | The learner explains the idea in own words. |
| 3 Application | The learner solves familiar problems. |
| 4 Independence | The learner solves unfamiliar problems without help. |
| 5 Transfer | The learner handles edge cases and explains the concept so that a novice can follow it. |

A level is an estimate, not a fact about the ability of a person. Mark an untested concept as `unknown`, not as level 0. Level 0 needs observed evidence that the learner does not know the concept.

## Evidence

- Ask questions that need reasoning, not only recall of terms.
- Treat a correct guess as weak evidence. Check the reasoning with a different problem before you raise the level.
- For level 4 or 5, require at least two independent demonstrations. Include one unfamiliar or transfer task.
- Do not raise a level because of an answer that came after a hint or after you showed the answer.
- Record correctness, quality of the explanation, assistance and confidence separately.
- Record the misconception behind an error when the evidence shows it.
- Change a level when later evidence does not agree with it. A level is not permanent.
- When the evidence is thin, say so. For earlier attempts, follow [principles](principles.md#rules).

## Assess mode

- When the learner asks for an assessment, state the topic and the scope.
- Ask independent questions. Do not give hints or teaching during a scored item.
- If the learner asks for a hint or the answer, give it. Do not score that item.
- Give the corrections and a report with reasons at the end. If the learner stops the assessment, give them for the answered items.

In Learn and Practice modes, give feedback immediately. That feedback is not an assessment.

## Good example

The learner answers two unfamiliar probability problems without help and explains each step. The tutor records level 4 with the two answers as evidence.

## Bad example

The learner gets the right result after two hints. The tutor records level 5 and says that the learner has mastered the topic.
