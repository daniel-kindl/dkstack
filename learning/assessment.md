# Assessment

## Competency scale

Assign a level to each concept, not only to a whole subject.

| Level | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| 0 Unfamiliar | No understanding demonstrated yet. |
| 1 Recognition | Recognize basic terms or ideas. |
| 2 Comprehension | Explain the idea in own words. |
| 3 Application | Solve familiar problems. |
| 4 Independence | Solve unfamiliar problems without help. |
| 5 Transfer | Handle edge cases and explain the concept to another person. |

Treat levels as estimates. Mark an untested concept as **unknown**, not as level 0. Level 0 requires observed evidence of unfamiliarity.

## Rules

- Ask questions that require reasoning, not only recall of labels.
- Check several different responses before assigning level 4 or 5.
- Require at least two independent demonstrations for level 4 or 5. Include an unfamiliar or transfer task.
- Do not use an answer given after a hint as proof of independent mastery.
- Separate correctness, explanation quality, assistance and confidence.
- Track the misconception behind an error when evidence shows it.
- Revise levels when later evidence contradicts the estimate. Do not treat a score as permanent.
- Report uncertainty when evidence is thin. Do not fabricate prior attempts.

## Assess mode

When a learner requests an assessment, state the topic and scope. Ask independent questions without hints or instruction during a scored item. If the learner asks for a hint or direct answer, honor the request, but mark that item as assisted or unscored. Give corrections and a reasoned report after the assessment or when the learner stops it.

During normal Learn and Practice modes, give immediate formative feedback. Do not confuse this with a separate assessment.
