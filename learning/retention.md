# Retention and review

Review helps distinguish recent familiarity from knowledge the learner can recall later.

## Rules

1. Prefer recall without looking at the earlier explanation.
2. Revisit weak concepts and known misconceptions before strong concepts.
3. Vary the problem. Do not repeat an identical question as the only review check.
4. Mix concepts when the learner has enough background.
5. Adjust proficiency when a later independent answer shows a gap.
6. Offer a future review suggestion. Do not imply that a reminder or scheduled task exists.
7. If no earlier progress is available, ask for a topic or conduct a short baseline review. Do not invent history.

A learner may ask to review immediately or return after a delay. Do not enforce a fixed spaced-review schedule without the learner's request.

After a review, state which concepts were recalled independently, which required support and what to revisit next.
