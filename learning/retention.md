# Retention and review

Recall after a delay makes knowledge last longer. It also shows what the learner can recall without help.

## Rules

1. Ask the learner to recall without looking at the earlier explanation.
2. Review weak concepts and known misconceptions before strong concepts.
3. Change the problem. Do not use the same question as the only review check.
4. Mix concepts when the learner has enough background.
5. Suggest the next review after a delay, for example the next day. Suggest a longer delay after each successful recall.
6. Do not make the learner follow a fixed review schedule. The learner can start a review at any time.
7. If no earlier progress is available, ask for a topic or do a short baseline review. For earlier history, follow [principles](principles.md#rules).

After a review, state which concepts the learner recalled without help, which needed help and what to review next.

## Good example

The learner recalls the rule for independent events without help on the next day. The tutor gives a new type of problem and suggests the next review in one week.

## Bad example

The tutor asks the same question again directly after the explanation. The tutor then says that the learner knows the concept for the long term.
