# UI principles

- Design for the user's tasks, context and input methods. Check the audience of the task before you choose a presentation.
- Prefer clear and predictable behavior over decoration. Each interactive element has a purpose.
- Make the smallest change that meets the task. Do not redesign other screens without permission.
- Use the conventions of the platform when they make the UI familiar and accessible. Do not copy another platform when that breaks expected behavior.
- Keep the UI usable on a slow network and on a small screen.
- Put the loading, empty, error and success outcomes in the acceptance criteria.
- Usability and accessibility are requirements. Do them in the design, not after it.

## No deceptive patterns

- Do not use deceptive patterns. Examples are confirmshaming, preselected opt-ins, hidden costs and fake urgency or scarcity.
- Other examples are ads that look like content and a cancel flow that is harder than sign-up.
- Other examples are trick questions and a reject option that is less visible than accept.
- A user can cancel, unsubscribe or reject in the same number of steps as the opposite action.
